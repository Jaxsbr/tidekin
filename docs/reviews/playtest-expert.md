# Expert agent playtest — baseline and beta comparison

**Status:** baseline, beta 2 (5180), and the beta 3 functional expedition candidate (5181) are played through. The final beta 3 results are in the last section; earlier scores and recommendations remain historical. The 5179 baseline run was captured before the canonical `dist` was rebuilt; its dated action log and screenshots remain the evidence for that earlier snapshot. This is an instrumented **agent-playtest**, not a human enjoyment report. The actions and captures show what the builds allowed and how feedback appeared; they cannot establish whether a person would find them fun.

## Baseline setup and earned progression

Played the stable production app at `http://127.0.0.1:5179/?qa=1` in system Chrome through Playwright with an isolated persistent profile. All progression came from visible UI, WASD steering, E interaction, Space diving/surfacing, journal claims, workshop build buttons, and legal chart travel. State/metrics reads were observational only. No resources, upgrades, or position were granted or edited.

The app at port 5179 has since been rebuilt to the current candidate. The baseline claim is limited to the state actually captured in the dated baseline folder below; reopening that URL now would not reproduce the baseline build.

The two uninterrupted play sessions totalled **10.1 in-game minutes** (603.1 game seconds) across **17.8 wall-clock minutes** (1,065.5 seconds). Session one ran 4m20s wall / 2m08s game and ended at 128.34 seconds with the waterwheel earned. After closing Chrome and reopening the same isolated profile, Continue restored the voyage at 128.62 seconds with the waterwheel, inventory, and stored power intact. Session two ran 13m26s wall / 7m55s game and ended at 603.33 seconds, day 4. Wall time includes observation and tool turn-around; it is not a player-speed estimate.

Earned route, in order:

1. Started at home with 4 wood and 1 scrap. Steered about 2.5 m to home driftwood and collected 4 wood. The journal's first salvage claim added 2 wood and 1 scrap.
2. Built the **waterwheel** (6 wood, 2 scrap). Claimed “Turn the tide” for 4 wood and 3 scrap. Over the next 25.8 game seconds with the game running, the wheel brought stored power from 0 to 34.7.
3. Steered to the home crate and collected 3 scrap. Built the **crane** (8 wood, 5 scrap), then claimed its journal milestone for 3 wood, 2 scrap, and 1 pearl. The crane's passive supply made later resource checks increasingly easy.
4. Collected 2 pearls from the home pearl bed through a real dive, claimed the pearl journal reward (3 wood, 1 scrap, 1 pearl), then built the **greenhouse** (8 wood, 4 scrap, 2 pearls) and **sail** (10 wood, 6 scrap, 2 pearls). These were sequential workshop choices, but the available order was strongly prescribed by their resource gates and journal rewards.
5. Used the chart to travel to Beacon Shoal/Horizon, steered to its wreck and collected 10 scrap, then made a real dive for 5 pearls. Built the **beacon** (12 wood, 10 scrap, 5 pearls) after returning home. At the build, inventory fell from 60 wood / 28 scrap / 5 pearls to 48 / 18 / 0.
6. Used chart travel to reach Mangrove Reach and Prism Reef, then steered to and gathered the mangrove timber (8 wood) and reef driftwood (6 wood). The chart showed all three islands discovered.

The final baseline state was all five upgrades true; discoveries `home`, `horizon`, `mangrove`, `reef`; direct gathering totals 18 wood, 13 scrap, and 7 pearls; 2 oxygen rescues; and 99 wood / 29 scrap / 0 pearls after passive production. Both rescues returned the diver home, restored oxygen, and preserved carried resources. The agent's long gaps between discrete dive inputs may have contributed to the rescues, so do not read that count as a human difficulty rate.

The learned efficient route is **wheel → claim its milestone → crane → claim → home pearls → claim → greenhouse + sail → Horizon wreck + pearls → beacon → chart to gather at Mangrove and Reef**. It succeeds because journal rewards bridge early recipes and the crane/greenhouse accrue materials while traveling or inspecting panels. That efficiency is also the loop's main pacing weakness: after the crane is built, passive income and repeated objective rewards reduce the pressure to choose which resource route to take. Chart travel is convenient and reliable, but it skips sailing between regions, so these island visits test destination activity more than the travel loop.

Tide was visible and at least one interaction changed with it. At Beacon Shoal, the salvage target indicated “Dive or wait for low tide”; after the tide cycled, the later captured state showed low tide and the salvage accessible. This demonstrates a clear binary access gate, but the screenshot landed after the gate opened. The wheel also converted the changing tide into power. The baseline HUD did not provide the new beta 2 countdown forecast, and the playtest did not establish a tide-dependent route choice or the cost of sailing through a shoal.

## Beta 2 blocking defects and next improvements (historical)

**No blocking playability defect was observed in these routes.** From a clean save, the game allowed the full earned upgrade arc, all island discoveries, an outside-pearl pickup, Beacon construction, and a successful two-session return. The two low-tide shoals remained traversable with both rigs; the fresh route needed no oxygen rescue. This does not certify untested high-tide, locator, failure-recovery, or device/performance cases, which belong to separate evidence.

The most valuable next change for core agency and tide depth is to make the existing shoals a readable route decision: on the current navigation/bearings UI, show the selected rig's estimated crossing time and remaining low-tide window, alongside an open-water bypass estimate. In the matched route the Skimmer took 16.52 seconds and retained 55 seconds before cover; the Pearl diver took 31.06 seconds and retained 41 seconds, yet both collected the same four pearls and both rigs had enough air. The forecast hints at a choice, but the UI does not yet help the player judge whether crossing now or going around is worth it. This is optional design depth, not a blocker.

Two other concrete opportunities are visible in the same evidence:

1. Give the Pearl diver a payoff on an intended pearl route where extra air matters. In this run the 12-second Skimmer also completed the immediate shallow pickup safely, so the 28-second air capacity did not alter the outcome.
2. Revisit early progression pacing after this successful route. All five upgrades and three island discoveries fit into 16.3 in-game seconds on the direct route; retain the legible Beacon shortfall but create more room for a deliberate resource-route or upgrade-order choice.

### Actual choices, research comparison, and remaining friction

The beta 2 finding that Skimmer dominated the tested pearl outing is addressed for the new Pearl trail: a 21-second one-breath route made the Diver's air useful, while Skimmer's 12-second capacity produced a safe failure. The player now has two useful early expedition goals with different preparation, rather than only one resource collection sequence. This supports the local research's proposed test that equipment should change what the player can complete, and that failure should preserve earned resources while costing opportunity. It does not establish human enjoyment or long-term retention. See [cozy systems research](../research/cozy-systems.md) and [cross-genre choice-depth hypotheses](../research/cross-genre.md#focused-hypotheses-for-the-next-playtest).

Tide also became a route decision in the observed play: the detour offer disappeared when the channels covered, returned when shoals emerged, and supplied a followable bend at low tide. I chose the displayed faster bypass on the repeat. This is stronger evidence than beta 2's countdown alone. Physical first arrival also gives sailing a purpose before the chart shortcut becomes available.

Remaining friction is design depth rather than an observed blocker:

- Both expedition circuits have fixed ordered markers. Once solved, Pearl trail largely becomes “fit Diver and repeat,” while the surface route retains more tide-dependent choice. No alternative underwater line or reward tradeoff was demonstrated.
- Daily completion counts and pennants provide a durable goal, but the repeated circuit gave the same reward. The UI did not record a best time or show how much the chosen route improved the outing. Long-term replay interest remains untested.
- Claims collapse the Expedition accordion, so viewing the updated title and both route availabilities requires reopening it. The daily label is clear, but gives no countdown to the next reward day. These are small usability costs, not progression failures.

**Most valuable next optional change:** record and show the last and best Current run completion time, with its rig and tide phase. The direct/around choice now works; a visible retained result would make route mastery part of the return loop instead of leaving its payoff only in an approximate ETA and the agent's log. This recommendation follows the actual repeat, not a score target. No app change was made by this playtester.
