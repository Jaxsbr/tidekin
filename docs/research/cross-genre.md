# Cross-genre reference scan: 10 Steam successes

## Scope and method

This scan covers Steam releases dated **8 October 2024 through 8 October 2026, inclusive**. It follows Tidekin’s project contract: a cheerful single-player raft workshop, island-hopping, tide reading, approachable diving and salvage, useful contraptions, gated destinations, and progression that persists between sessions ([project brief](../PROJECT.md)). The selected games’ central activities are action, roguelike, strategy, puzzle, horror, or management. I classified by the activity described by the developer and Steam store, not every broad user tag; the set avoids games centered on survival crafting, cozy play, or factory automation.

Steam review counts and language-specific scores below are an **8 October 2026 retrieval snapshot**, rounded where useful because Steam counts change and some returned page variants differ slightly. They indicate player response and reach, not sales or unique players. Where available, I add a developer/publisher sales or access milestone and label it precisely. The captured Steam observations are preserved in [cross-genre-evidence.json](cross-genre-evidence.json); if retrieved variants conflict, I report the disagreement instead of selecting a convenient score. These examples are selected because they show meaningful traction; their commercial results do **not** prove that any individual mechanic caused that result. The loop breakdowns are design readings of the games’ published mechanics, not causal findings.

## 1. Hollow Knight: Silksong — action / exploration

**Evidence.** Released 4 September 2025. The observed Steam page showed 146,338 English reviews, 93% positive. Team Cherry later reported that over seven million people had purchased it by 15 December 2025; Game Pass players were reported separately. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1030300/Hollow_Knight_Silksong/) · [Team Cherry launch post](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/silksong-available-now) · [Team Cherry sales update](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/holiday2025).

**Loop and recovery.** In a minute, read an enemy’s tell, move, strike or use a tool, then respond to the hit or opening. A session can map a new region, reach a checkpoint, complete a quest, or learn a boss. Across sessions, acquired abilities and tools open routes back through a connected world; quests and optional challenges give detours a purpose. Enemy tells and crisp hit reactions feed back immediately, while route, tool, and quest choices shape what the player attempts next. A failed fight costs time and another attempt; checkpoints and accumulated map knowledge make recovery legible rather than erasing the broader expedition. Team Cherry’s feature overview highlights exploration, acrobatic combat, tools, quests, bosses, and an optional harder mode. [Official feature reveal](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/hollow-knight-silksong-revealed).

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow the feeling that a new tool changes how familiar places can be crossed: a salvage hook, better fins, or an engine part could reveal a safe route through a reef or tide gate. Keep the map’s information and earned upgrades after a failed dive. The risk is importing combat difficulty and backtracking into a relaxed travel fantasy. **Hypothesis:** after earning one traversal upgrade, players will choose to revisit a previously seen island route and can explain what the upgrade made newly possible.

## 2. Path of Exile 2 — action RPG

**Evidence.** Entered Steam Early Access on 6 December 2024. The observed Steam page showed 97,431 English reviews, 77% positive. Grinding Gear Games said one million Early Access redemptions had been reached before launch; this is an access/redemption count, not a sales figure. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2694490/Path_of_Exile_2/) · [GGG’s one-million-redemptions announcement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-iFva8e6PhA).

**Loop and recovery.** The minute loop is real-time movement, attacks and skills against readable enemy patterns, followed by equipment or loot decisions. A session advances a campaign zone, boss, or endgame map. Across sessions, character levels, gear, skill gems, and passive-tree choices create a build that changes how later encounters play. The important choice is not just which reward to take, but which skills and supports to combine and which defenses to sacrifice. Enemy telegraphs, damage numbers, drops, and skill effects make cause and result visible. Failure generally means another attempt with the character’s broader campaign and build intact, though the exact cost depends on the Early Access activity and its rules. The game’s Steam page describes a large skill system, boss encounters, campaign, and endgame mapping; those systems are still in development.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow build identity at a much smaller scale: let a few workshop modules combine in legible ways, such as a stronger paddlewheel improving both speed and salvage reach. Offer a clear preview of what a part changes. The risk is overwhelming a browser-game audience with inventory, stats, and build arithmetic. **Hypothesis:** players can choose between two contrasting raft loadouts, predict their practical difference, and notice that difference during a short island trip without consulting a guide.

## 3. DOOM: The Dark Ages — action FPS

**Evidence.** Steam lists 14 May 2025; Bethesda/id Software’s launch coverage gives 15 May, a one-day storefront/time-zone discrepancy. Both dates fall inside the window. The observed Steam page showed 19,854 English reviews, 86% positive. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3017860/DOOM_The_Dark_Ages/) · [Bethesda/id Software Developer Direct](https://slayersclub.bethesda.net/en-US/news/doom-the-dark-ages-developer-direct).

**Loop and recovery.** Moment to moment, identify the immediate threat and switch between attack, movement, and the Shield Saw’s block, parry, throw, or deflection uses. A level strings those encounters together with optional exploration and resource pickups; upgrades and discoveries carry forward through the campaign. Threat cues, parry timing, and strong weapon responses make feedback immediate. Players can choose which branches and secrets to pursue, and difficulty settings adjust the pressure. A failed encounter returns the player to a checkpoint; Bethesda’s exploration guidance also describes checkpoints, extra-life pickups, and an automap. [Shield Saw tips](https://slayersclub.bethesda.net/en-US/news/doom-the-dark-ages-shield-saw-tips) · [Exploration tips](https://slayersclub.bethesda.net/en-US/news/doom-the-dark-ages-exploration-tips) · [Progression tips](https://slayersclub.bethesda.net/en-US/news/doom-the-dark-ages-progression-tips).

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow multifunctional interaction and unmistakable feedback, not violence: one raft tool might catch salvage, brace against a surge, or turn a waterwheel when timed well. Let the animation and sound confirm a successful action, and use generous checkpoints around demanding dives. The risk is making every ocean event feel like a fight or adding twitch demands to an approachable dive. **Hypothesis:** in a short test, players will recognize the same tool’s two uses and report that a mistimed action was understandable and recoverable.

## 4. Amanda the Adventurer 2 — horror / puzzle adventure

**Evidence.** Released 22 October 2024. The observed Steam page showed 1,434 all-language reviews, 83% positive. The store describes a larger library setting, interactive cartoons, puzzles, and choices that affect the story. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2826800/Amanda_the_Adventurer_2/) · [developer’s library-puzzle update](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/2826800/announcements/detail/4447961335474421841).

**Loop and recovery.** A short beat is watching a tape, spotting a clue, choosing how to respond, or finding an item a character requests. A play session alternates tape puzzles with exploring the library for objects and story clues. Across sessions, collected information builds the player’s picture of the characters and the mystery. The meaningful choice is often whether to cooperate with a request, refuse it, or investigate its context; the environment and dialogue answer those choices. Feedback comes from altered tape content, puzzle outcomes, and newly understood story details. When a clue is missed, the player can search the room and revisit the tape; the developer’s puzzle-overhaul announcement also shows that puzzle legibility and room navigation needed adjustment after release.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Use island characters and found objects to tell a small, cheerful mystery: an artifact can prompt a request, and helping or declining can change what a villager reveals. Keep clue review in a local journal and ensure players can recover from a missed clue without losing a long voyage. The obvious risk is importing unsettling imagery or opaque escape-room logic into a bright game. **Hypothesis:** after finding one object and hearing two different responses, players will understand that their choice changed the next conversation and will want to learn why.

## 5. Blue Prince — puzzle / strategy

**Evidence.** Released 10 April 2025. The observed Steam page showed 14,768 English reviews, 85% positive. Raw Fury’s official description explains the room-drafting structure, daily reset, and permanent estate improvements. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1569580/Blue_Prince/) · [Raw Fury game page](https://rawfury.com/games/blue-prince/).

**Loop and recovery.** Each door creates a near-term decision: draft a room whose resources, exits, or special function suit the current plan. A session is an attempt to reach the mansion’s goal before the day’s limited steps run out. Across sessions, permanent improvements and player knowledge make future attempts more informed even though the drafted layout resets. Room choice is meaningful because it changes route options and the supplies available later; the map and room effects provide immediate feedback. A failed day resets the layout, but not all accumulated progress, turning failure into another attempt with better information rather than a total wipe.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Treat islands as route choices with distinct benefits and costs: a short stop for common salvage, a longer route for a rare part, or a tide-dependent passage with a useful shortcut. Carry forward charts, discovered landmarks, and earned upgrades when a trip ends early. The risk is making tide timing a hidden timer or turning exploration into route optimization alone. **Hypothesis:** when offered two visible island routes with different costs and rewards, players will choose for a stated reason and later adjust that choice after learning the tide.

## 6. The Roottrees are Dead — investigation puzzle

**Evidence.** Released 15 January 2025. The observed Steam page showed 5,147 English reviews, 96% positive. The official Steam description centers on researching photographs, books, and articles to establish blood relationships and complete a family tree. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2754380/The_Roottrees_are_Dead/).

**Loop and recovery.** The minute loop is searching an in-game early-internet archive, comparing a clue, and placing or revising a person on the evidence board. A session develops one or more branches of the family tree; across sessions, the player’s accumulated deductions narrow the remaining possibilities and suggest new searches. Progress is primarily knowledge rather than character power. Evidence is the feedback: a source supports or rules out a relationship, and the growing tree makes gaps visible. The player chooses which lead to pursue and how to reconcile conflicting clues. Mistakes are recoverable by revisiting evidence and changing a hypothesis; the design depends on making the correction path clear enough that uncertainty feels intriguing rather than arbitrary.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Let salvage and island conversations populate a ship’s log or chart: an old marker, a current pattern, and a villager’s clue can combine to reveal a new safe route or resource site. Keep notes and known facts visible in-game, so players never need external spreadsheets. The risk is making a colorful exploration game feel like desk research. **Hypothesis:** after collecting three clues, players can infer and navigate to one previously unknown feature without a direct waypoint marker, while still describing what evidence led them there.

## 7. Mewgenics — tactical roguelike / strategy

**Evidence.** Released 10 February 2026. The observed Steam variants showed about 28,800 English reviews, but disagreed between 90% and 91% positive; I leave the percentage unavailable. Its store description lays out the home-base, squad, turn-based expedition, cat traits, equipment, and breeding systems. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/686060/Mewgenics/).

**Loop and recovery.** In each tactical turn, position a small squad, choose attacks or abilities, and respond to enemy threats. A session sends a selected team through an expedition and returns with experience, items, scars, or other consequences. Across sessions, the home and future squads change through upgrades and inherited traits. Squad composition and in-combat positioning are meaningful because roles and abilities interact; the grid and explicit turn order make consequences easy to read. Failure can leave lasting damage or remove a run’s momentum, while home-base systems and replacement teams let the wider campaign continue. The game’s layered systems give repeated expeditions variety, but also raise the cognitive load.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow complementary roles and visible trade-offs at a much lighter scale: choose which two tools or helpers to bring on a dive, with each solving a different problem. Persistent upgrades can make a later attempt easier without making a single lost item fatal. Do not import animal breeding, character loss, or the dense tactics layer; those are poor fits for Tidekin’s gentle recovery promise. **Hypothesis:** players can explain why one of two tool combinations suits a particular island, and a failed dive still leaves a useful route or resource insight.

## 8. Slay the Spire 2 — roguelike deckbuilder / strategy

**Evidence.** Entered Steam Early Access on 5 March 2026. The observed Steam page showed about 69,000 English reviews and a 91% positive adjusted lifetime score, while explicitly flagging periods of off-topic review activity; its recent-review score was 70% positive across 4,594 reviews in one retrieved snapshot. Mega Crit reported three million units sold and over 25 million runs attempted after roughly one week. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2868840/Slay_the_Spire_2/) · [Mega Crit’s March 2026 sales announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/2868840/view/502852451136700526).

**Loop and recovery.** The minute loop is selecting a card against an enemy’s visible intent, managing energy, and seeing the result. A run is a route through fights, events, shops, and rests toward a boss; across runs, players unlock content, learn enemy patterns, and try different characters and builds. Card rewards, route selection, and resource spending create consequential choices, while enemy intent and combat math explain why a choice worked or failed. A lost run resets its temporary deck and route, but not the player’s knowledge or all unlocked content. The sequel’s Early Access announcement promises continued balancing and content additions, so the snapshot describes a changing game.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow compact planning choices and visible hazard intent, not literal cards or a run wipe: before a dive, show a few actions or tools and clearly telegraph the tide, current, or depth hazard. A failed trip should return durable salvage and knowledge. The risk is abstracting away the tactile 3D sailing and diving that define Tidekin. **Hypothesis:** a visible forecast plus a small preparation choice will change players’ route or equipment selection, and they can explain the choice before launch.

## 9. Tempest Rising — real-time strategy

**Evidence.** Steam’s store page lists 24 April 2025, while its official announcement says the game unexpectedly became publicly available on 17 April after the planned editions unlocked early; 3D Realms later described opening the game to everyone as the smoothest solution. The observed Steam page showed 5,301 English reviews, 87% positive; recent reviews were Mixed at 69% across 166 reviews in the same returned snapshot. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1486920/Tempest_Rising/) · [official availability announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1486920/view/505074444233867634).

**Loop and recovery.** In the moment, scout, place units, issue orders, and respond to threats while watching limited resources. A session is a mission or multiplayer match whose map, objectives, and faction shape the opening plan. Across sessions, campaign progress and familiarity with unit roles broaden the player’s strategic options. The consequential choices are where to spend resources, which units to field, and when to defend or press an advantage. Unit orders, combat effects, and resource changes give fast feedback. A tactical setback can be answered by retreating, rebuilding, or changing the next push; a lost mission usually requires a retry, and poor early decisions can compound quickly. The official Steam description frames it as a classic RTS about factions competing for power and resources.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow prioritization during a short island event: choose whether to secure the raft, rescue salvage, or take a risky shortcut while the tide shifts. Keep the choice brief and make the consequence visible instead of introducing a production chain or constant unit micromanagement. The risk is turning a relaxing voyage into a high-pressure strategy timer. **Hypothesis:** players facing one simultaneous two-part island problem will identify a priority, see the cost of that choice, and still feel able to recover afterward.

## 10. Jurassic World Evolution 3 — management strategy

**Evidence.** Released 21 October 2025. The observed Steam page showed about 7,400 English reviews, 93% positive. Frontier’s official launch announcement describes campaign, sandbox, and challenge modes; its campaign overview says players build and manage parks across locations and balance security, entertainment, and conservation interests. [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2958130/Jurassic_World_Evolution_3/) · [Frontier’s exact launch announcement](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/648350/view/527609017446434568) · [official game-mode overview](https://www.jurassicworldevolution.com/en-US/3/game-modes).

**Loop and recovery.** The short loop is placing or adjusting a facility, checking its effect on animals, guests, or operations, and correcting an emerging problem. A session works toward campaign objectives or an open-ended park target. Across sessions, locations, species, research, and park layouts extend the management space. The player chooses which stakeholder needs and park investments to prioritize; visitor response, animal behavior, and operational alerts show the consequences. Maintenance, veterinary, and security responses provide ways to correct trouble rather than requiring a full restart. The campaign’s competing interests provide structure beyond decorating or maximizing one score.

**Tidekin transfer, risk, and test.** Borrow the readable “I changed the place, and now it behaves differently” loop: a workshop upgrade should visibly alter raft handling, salvage access, or how a familiar island responds. Use one or two competing needs at a time and let players correct an imperfect setup. Avoid adding a large staff roster, upkeep ledger, or idle production layer. **Hypothesis:** after one upgrade, returning to the same island produces a noticeable behavior or access change that players can describe without interpreting a stack of meters.

## Synthesis for Tidekin: choice depth, risks, and tests

- **Keep recovery productive.** On failure, retain something useful: chart knowledge, a checkpoint, durable salvage, or a clearer understanding of a hazard. This aligns with Tidekin’s requirement that missed tides and oxygen depletion not create dead ends.
- **Make choices visible before commitment.** Blue Prince and Slay the Spire 2 show how route and resource decisions gain meaning when players can see likely costs; Tidekin can expose tide windows, current direction, and route rewards without removing uncertainty.
- **Let upgrades change behavior.** The strongest transfer from action, RPG, and management systems is a tool or workshop part that changes traversal and interaction, not just a larger number.
- **Use distinct scales of play.** A quick action, a short island outing, and persistent workshop progress can support one another, while keeping the core loop tactile and readable.
- **Treat every transfer as a test.** The hypotheses above should be measured with observation and playtests—choice comprehension, recovery behavior, route changes, and stated reasons—not treated as explanations for another game’s sales.

### Choice depth in the current draft

The current design snapshot supplied for this review is a **180-second tide cycle, an 18-second dive/rescue window, rescue with no resource loss, five workshop upgrades, three destinations, and objectives**. On paper, that is a clear, appropriately small MVP loop, but its choice depth is **light unless the options change what the player can do or reach**. A moving tide is a decision only when it changes route access, salvage, or safe return; otherwise it is atmosphere. An 18-second dive/rescue window can create a sharp moment, but if the player has only one obvious action before rescue, it is a countdown rather than a choice. No-loss rescue is a strong match for the project’s recovery promise, but meaningful stakes then need to come from opportunity cost—time, missed access, or a different reward—not losing earned resources.

Five upgrades provide progression, but if all are mandatory in a fixed order, they are a checklist rather than a build choice. Three destinations provide a world map, but if objectives and gates prescribe one sequence, route choice is similarly thin. The design does not need more systems to fix this: make at least two upgrades or destinations plausibly useful at a given point, give their benefits distinct consequences, and show those consequences before commitment. Keep objectives as direction, with room for a player to choose the route or order of work.

### Adaptation risks to guard against

- **Action games:** copying combat cadence can make the raft and diving feel twitchy or hostile. Preserve fast, readable feedback but use it for steering, salvage, and recovery.
- **Roguelikes and deckbuilders:** copying run resets or elaborate build arithmetic conflicts with persistent progress and the small upgrade budget. Borrow short planning decisions and synergies; keep earned progress durable and combinations easy to read.
- **Puzzle and horror games:** copying opaque clues, failure gates, or unsettling tone can turn exploration into frustration or break the cheerful art direction. Keep clues local and reviewable, and make mystery optional and warm in tone.
- **RTS and management games:** copying simultaneous emergencies, staff needs, meters, and upkeep would add pressure and busywork. Limit each outing to one clear trade-off and make each workshop upgrade change play in a visible way.
- **Tide and dive timers:** 180 seconds and 18 seconds can become arbitrary clocks if players cannot read what is about to change. Forecast the relevant tide state and make the dive/rescue window legible; test whether players have enough time for more than one meaningful action without rushing through the scene.

### Focused hypotheses for the next playtest

1. **Tide choice:** before a 180-second cycle changes, players can predict one practical consequence and choose between two routes for a stated reason. If not, improve the forecast or make the tide change alter access more clearly.
2. **Dive choice and recovery:** within the 18-second window before no-loss rescue, players can choose between at least two useful actions; rescue feels safe, while the time or missed opportunity still matters. Observe whether they understand why the dive ended and willingly try again.
3. **Upgrade and destination choice:** at one progression point, players choose between two viable upgrades or destinations, explain the difference, and later notice the choice shaping a route or objective. If everyone picks the same option because only one is useful, the system offers progression but not meaningful choice.

These are design hypotheses for Tidekin’s current draft. The genre examples establish that the games exist, their release dates, their Steam response, and the mechanics described by their creators; they do not show that importing any one mechanic will improve Tidekin or explain those games’ commercial outcomes.
