# Cozy systems research for Tidekin

Research completed **8 October 2026**. Eligible release window: **8 October 2024–8 October 2026, inclusive**. Tidekin context supplied for this assignment: an **ocean, raft, tide, and contraption game**. Transfers below are proposals. The dedicated current-loop audit separately identifies behavior verified in a dated source snapshot.

## Evidence and selection

This is a purposive comparison of ten games with demonstrable audience traction across survival crafting, automation, exploration, and cozy play. Here, “successful” means substantial positive Steam reception: at least 1,000 Steam-purchaser reviews and a positive majority. This is an explicit reception criterion, **not a profitability claim**. Forever Skies additionally has a dated official sales milestone; Luma Island has an official player milestone. Neither metric is inferred from review counts.

Only primary material supports factual claims: Steam store descriptions, Steam review data and individual reviews, and developer/publisher announcements. Developer-authored Steam announcements were read through Valve's public news API when the community page's text was unavailable, and their redirect links were resolved to the canonical announcement pages cited below. No fan wiki, press review, sales estimator, or unverified future release is used as evidence.

The comparable review snapshot was retrieved **2026-10-08, 08:49:20 UTC**—21:49 NZDT—with `language=all`, `purchase_type=steam`, `filter=all`, and `num_per_page=1`. Counts below are `query_summary.total_reviews`; percentages are `total_positive / total_reviews`, rounded to one decimal. These are cumulative totals, not reviews written only during the eligibility window. The legacy endpoint returned these data successfully; Valve now marks it deprecated and documents its replacement and matching filters in [IUserReviewsService](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/IUserReviewsService). Off-topic activity filtering was left at the endpoint's default; these are not an independently reconstructed unfiltered census.

Individual feedback was sampled separately from English reviews, both recommendations and non-recommendations, using the helpfulness-oriented `filter=all`, `day_range=365`, and `purchase_type=all`. Two or three reviews of each polarity were requested per game; selected examples are linked individually. This small, deliberately contrasted sample identifies design questions, **not complaint prevalence**. Review dates below are original posting dates in UTC; text and recommendation status were observed on 8 October 2026 and may have been edited subsequently. A positive recommendation can contain substantial criticism.

**Causality boundary:** these mechanics coexist with reception evidence. This research does not establish that any mechanic caused sales or positive reviews. Existing franchises, price, presentation, marketing, release maturity, and audience expectations are confounders. Proposed experiments test usefulness for Tidekin, not explanations of another game's commercial performance. Loop descriptions reconstruct relationships among documented actions; their timings and experiential effects were not measured in playtests.

## 1. Aloft — environmental energy and a traveling home

**Release and traction.** Astrolabe Interactive's game entered Early Access on 15 January 2025. The dated snapshot above records 3,078 Steam-purchaser reviews, 82.6% positive. This supports meaningful reception, not proven profitability. Its [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1660080/Aloft/) still identifies it as Early Access.

**Documented loop.** Choose an island → build a helm, rudder, and sails → sail or glide to other islands → collect resources and knowledge → bring seeds and animals home → craft and expand the traveling base. Wind can serve both navigation and production: windmills, cables, and pulleys power automated machines; stronger winds increase production and sailing speed while testing sails. Food provides buffs. This couples the location of the home to what its machinery can do. [Developer's store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1660080/Aloft/).

**Progression and feedback.** The shipped **Bloom & Blight**, 4 November 2025, separates knowledge, sailing, and research progression. Restoring three Life Saplings in a sector enables healing its Life Tree; the tree's basin supplies Insight for further technology. Restoration also opens fast wind routes. The detailed changelog specifies flora, fauna, and funga requirements, obstacle removal, a Life Wind activity, and different healthy/unhealthy environmental audio. Recovery therefore has visible, navigational, and progression consequences. These are later systems, not descriptions of the January launch build. [Update announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1660080/announcements/detail/500587968372670787), [shipped changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1660080/announcements/detail/613178682495730977).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** A [25 January 2026 non-recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198004417947/recommended/1660080/) praises the mobile ecosystem clinic but reports that the revised unlock sequence forced backtracking and reduced agency. A [5 May 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197992865463/recommended/1660080/) finds the islands too small for satisfying exploration. A [1 February recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198054977998/recommended/1660080/) enjoys the game while questioning its ecological framing. These are individual accounts, and the later funga restoration requirements complicate any blanket claim that the game treats all fungi as undesirable. The developer also confirmed a concrete recovery issue: the [11 September 2026 patch](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1660080/announcements/detail/719041721925632357) lets heat-struck players swing tools to repair sails and escape hot areas. A survival debuff must not prevent the action required to recover from it.

**Tidekin transfer hypothesis and risk.** Let the raft's place in the ocean affect its machines: strong tidal flow turns a wheel; slack water provides a safe workshop; a restored reef opens a sheltered channel. Make ecological improvement observable before awarding a technology point. The risk is mandatory restoration becoming a repeated checklist, particularly if players pass useful salvage they are artificially forbidden to use. Offer several routes to the same capability and let players record discoveries for later use.

**Concrete experiment — E1, one wheel and two channels.** In a 25-minute graybox, offer a fast exposed channel and a slow sheltered one, one tidal wheel, and one reef repair that creates a third route. Test eight players, including four who prefer cozy games. Pass if six can explain how current affects output, five voluntarily revisit the repaired reef, and no player is unable to recover a stopped raft. Log route choices and forced waits. Revise if success requires explaining an invisible unlock sequence.

## 2. Forever Skies — the mobile base is both investment and vulnerability

**Release and traction.** Far From Home's [14 April 2025 1.0 announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1641960/announcements/detail/538850173472735859) adds a story finale, defined biomes, locations, tools, and quality-of-life changes. Its [19 January 2026 announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1641960/announcements/detail/312570398570973973) reports **over 500,000 copies sold** cumulatively; it does not provide a platform breakdown. The Steam snapshot is 8,158 reviews, 77.9% positive. This is commercially evidenced traction with qualified sentiment, not uniformly strong acclaim.

**Documented loop.** Pilot the airship to ruins → leave the ship to scavenge and scan → analyze and reverse-engineer technology → convert resources into tools and ship improvements → use the improved traveling workshop to reach further locations. The ship combines shelter, laboratory, and storage with movement. Hull integrity and hostile surroundings complicate travel; exploration supplies both material and information. [Developer's store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1641960/Forever_Skies/).

**Progression and feedback.** The 1.0 release uses biome barriers, new research parts, collectible artifacts/murals, and cosmetic rewards. Its radar gained biome and location information; discovery counts and a saving indicator expose important state. Ingredient auto-crafting lets printers produce missing intermediates when raw inputs are available. Later, **Final Echoes**, 27 July 2026, introduced ship electricity: an initial 200-unit allowance, generators, upgrades, and a damage overhaul under which insufficient power or unprotected lightning can destroy ship parts and devices. This is a major later change in the relationship between expansion and safety. [1.0 notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1641960/announcements/detail/538850173472735859), [Final Echoes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1641960/announcements/detail/698771083706239636).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** A [4 November 2025 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199796684825/recommended/1641960/) enjoyed the game but stopped when its ship became permanently stuck. The January 2026 patch explicitly fixes a rare stuck-airship case after loading; this corroborates the failure class, not a claim that every instance is solved. A [14 November recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198068709300/recommended/1641960/) reports tedious deposit operations and difficulty identifying the right crafting device. A [27 July 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198115239266/recommended/1641960/) describes an update disrupting an established ship, then praises the developer's rapid response; a [4 August review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561199242048193/recommended/1641960/) criticizes research gates and repeated points of interest. The enduring design question is how to add depth without invalidating a player's home or multiplying errands.

**Tidekin transfer hypothesis and risk.** Traveling with the workshop can make every found part relevant to an immediately visible project. Keep the return-from-expedition flow brief: unload through one interaction, craft from raft storage, preview any weight/power effect before placement. Use repairable local damage with an explicit rescue operation. A total raft loss can remove travel, crafting, stored progress, and personal expression simultaneously; that compound loss is especially risky for cozy positioning.

**Concrete experiment — E2, grounded raft recovery.** Give eight players a raft they decorate, then introduce a sandbar and a disabled power module in a 30-minute session. Compare full material refunds plus tow-to-safe-water against repair with partial salvage loss; counterbalance order. Pass only if everyone can recover within two minutes without restarting, six correctly predict expansion's power cost, and at least six still want another expedition. Measure frustration immediately after recovery. Preserve the raft if loss produces resignation instead of a new plan.

## 3. Oddsparks: An Automation Adventure — visible workers make logistics tangible

**Release and traction.** Massive Miniteam's game left Early Access on **27 May 2025**. Its [official 1.0 account](https://oddsparks.thqnordicmobile.com/en/oddsparks-enters-1-0/) describes a final biome, tier-ten quests, a capstone automation challenge, and extensive new controls and tools. The Steam snapshot is 1,874 reviews, 82.3% positive.

**Documented loop.** Explore for resources and ruins → clear space → build workshops → assign Sparks to carrying and crafting → connect workshops through paths → satisfy village quests → unlock more buildings and expand. Sparks themselves can be manufactured. Transport is worker traffic rather than conveyor belts, so distance, elevation, and congestion become layout problems. Adventures and production share the same recognizable creatures. [Developer's Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1817800/Oddsparks_An_Automation_Adventure/).

**Progression and feedback.** The 1.0 additions include sensors, controllable gates, better filter modes, and remote train management. Recipe interfaces display both duration and items per minute. A hostility overlay previews danger before building; corruption can stop workshops and paths, while Scrubby Sparks clean affected structures. World presets can grant automation tools earlier, change resource demands, or alter combat. This makes both process state and impending failure inspectable. The 1.0 release deliberately broke old-save compatibility and provided a legacy branch. [Developer's launch notes](https://oddsparks.thqnordicmobile.com/en/oddsparks-enters-1-0/).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** An [18 April 2026 recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197966113653/recommended/1817800/) enjoys the relaxed factory and trains but finds some advanced logic unnecessary because simpler alternatives suffice. A [26 October 2025 recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198032301932/recommended/1817800/) appreciates visual conditional routing. In contrast, a [30 November 2025 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198017374263/recommended/1817800/) objects to large resource demands, regional manufacturing requirements, and repeated restructuring; a [27 March 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198175631642/recommended/1817800/) dislikes restricted workshop placement. The former reviewer characterizes the capstone as a restart, whereas the developer describes outside production feeding it. Treat the dissatisfaction as evidence of perceived discontinuity, not proof that all prior work is mechanically discarded.

**Tidekin transfer hypothesis and risk.** Build a contraption vocabulary players can watch: float rises → cord pulls → gate opens → water fills a basin → output becomes available. Advanced parts should solve a real problem encountered in a simpler arrangement. Tide reversal can justify a one-way valve; two outputs can justify a splitter. Risks include introducing logic before the need exists, making attractive designs inefficient by accident, and requiring a complete rebuild to change scale. Provide movable modules and clear bottleneck state.

**Concrete experiment — E3, diagnose a stalled machine.** Give ten players a functioning tide-powered collector, then separately cause insufficient water head, a full output bin, and a closed gate. Compare animated motion alone with motion plus a short contextual status label. Pass if eight diagnose each case within 45 seconds and seven solve a later routing task using a new valve without a step-by-step tutorial. Log which parts players inspect. If motion is pleasant but uninformative, retain it and improve state communication before adding more recipes.

## 4. Abiotic Factor — discoveries should change the toolkit

**Release and traction.** Deep Field Games released **1.0 on 22 July 2025**, confirmed by its [launch announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/games/427410/announcements/detail/637938513402135173). The [Cold Fusion update](https://steamcommunity.com/games/427410/announcements/detail/543363541685502088) adds sectors, progression, equipment enhancement, and other systems. The Steam snapshot is 42,391 reviews, 95.9% positive; this includes its earlier Early Access period.

**Documented loop.** Scavenge an office or laboratory → turn its contents into a base and unconventional equipment → use traps and tools against threats → explore the facility and portal worlds → bring useful materials home → improve gear and logistics for the next excursion. Players choose scientific specializations and traits, develop skills, and can relocate possessions using carts, vehicles, or teleporters. The base is a practical response to exploration rather than just a place to store trophies. [Developer's store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/427410/Abiotic_Factor/).

**Progression and feedback.** Cold Fusion adds an Enhancement Bench for selected weapons, armor, shields, and backpacks; skill levels extend to twenty. A Database Terminal's Item Manager retrieves items from local containers. The GATEPal lists resources associated with traders, exchanges, and portal worlds. Specialized backpack slots address refrigeration and radiation. These changes make previous discoveries easier to reuse and make equipment progression include new functions, not just damage increases. The launch guidance also documents configurable sandbox settings, rebinding, and a location-pinging pager. [Cold Fusion notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/427410/announcements/detail/543363541685502088), [launch guidance](https://steamcommunity.com/games/427410/announcements/detail/637938513402135173).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** An [8 April 2026 recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197985098213/recommended/427410/) values the combination of survival crafting, setting, and story. A [2 December 2025 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198096172583/recommended/427410/) praises early secrets and components that reveal new craftable things, but finds later areas sparse and tedious. A [7 June 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198012113435/recommended/427410/) reports repetition overwhelming its enjoyment late in the campaign. A [18 October 2025 solo review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198020753198/recommended/427410/) objects to supply journeys. These suggest testing both discovery density and solo logistics; strong cumulative sentiment does not eliminate late-game friction.

**Tidekin transfer hypothesis and risk.** Let the first salvaged component demonstrate a previously impossible action: a lens reads a reef signal, a flexible seal makes a reversible pump, or a buoy identifies submerged salvage. Record where materials were found, and let the player pin the next machine's requirements. Reuse parts in several inventions so an old discovery remains relevant. The risk is a long ocean crossing to acquire a component that produces only a small statistical upgrade. Horror and combat are not prerequisites for transferring this discovery/tool relationship.

**Concrete experiment — E4, capability versus quantity.** Build two otherwise comparable three-island paper/graybox routes: one grants three progressively larger storage capacities; the other grants pumping, sensing, and towing. Test ten solo players for 30 minutes, counterbalancing routes. Pass the capability hypothesis if seven accurately describe a newly possible plan and at least six choose to revisit an earlier island for a newly usable opportunity. Compare travel time per useful discovery. Reduce distance or change rewards if the session's main remembered event is another supply run.

## 5. Dinkum — a settlement makes accumulated work socially visible

**Release and traction.** James Bendon's official launch update was scheduled for **23 April 2025, 02:00 UTC** and announced applied shortly afterward; Steam metadata instead says 22 April. The release added Creative Mode, multiplayer permissions/improvements, and shared mounts. The Steam snapshot is 24,666 reviews, 92.4% positive. [Launch notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1062520/announcements/detail/511829843667583197), [applied announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1062520/announcements/detail/511829843667583580), [store metadata](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1062520/Dinkum/).

**Documented loop.** Forage, fish, grow crops, or explore mines → sell goods for Dinks → invest in the town → attract shops and visitors → develop a larger farm/workshop and improve equipment → choose another activity. Seasons and weather affect crops. Town layout, furniture, character customization, animals, and festivals offer persistent expression alongside dangerous wildlife and mining. [Developer's Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1062520/Dinkum/).

**Progression and feedback.** Growth becomes visible through buildings and residents as well as possessions. The launch's Town Bell temporarily marks villagers on everyone's map, turning a social-search task into an explicit navigational aid. A Bug Terrarium produces cocoons that can be spun into silk, linking collecting, care, processing, and construction. Creative Mode can change time/weather and spawn items or animals, but permanently disables achievements for that save; the documentation states that boundary. [Official launch patch notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1062520/announcements/detail/511829843667583197).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** A [28 November 2025 recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198219237777/recommended/1062520/) describes turning an orchard's income into residents, farming, animals, and a decorated workshop; it also reports needing external explanations for poorly surfaced mechanics. A [13 April 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198320569446/recommended/1062520/) reports multiplayer disconnects losing a guest's entire day's progress and inventory. That is a particular player's account, not a claim that current co-op universally loses progress. Other sampled negatives focus on publisher policies; this report does not infer policy allegations as gameplay flaws or treat them as explanations of the aggregate score.

**Tidekin transfer hypothesis and risk.** Tie raft investment to recurring people: a repaired visiting skiff returns, a shared dock hosts a new trade, or a rescued craftsperson visibly uses the player's mechanism. Let beauty, usefulness, and social continuity provide distinct reasons to keep building. Keep basic construction and creative expression available early; towns can otherwise become another chain of permissions before players reach what they came to do. If co-op enters scope, expedition and inventory persistence must survive a guest disconnect independently of a day boundary.

**Concrete experiment — E5, one returning neighbor.** In a three-session storyboard or lightweight prototype, compare an anonymous delivery board with one named sailor whose boat changes after each delivered part. Give eight players the same materials and rewards. Pass if six remember what their work enabled and five choose an optional non-paying repair. Ask them to point to a visible consequence rather than recite dialogue. A later technical co-op trial should require disconnect/rejoin to preserve every committed inventory transaction; that reliability criterion is separate from the social hypothesis.

## 6. Luma Island — remove handling limits without removing meaningful choices

**Release and traction.** Feel Free Games released Luma Island on **20 November 2024**. The Steam snapshot records 11,272 reviews, 84.1% positive. Its [24 September 2026 press release](https://www.lumaisland.com/press-2026-09-24) reports **more than 800,000 PC players**. This is a player count, not a copies-sold statement. Console versions in that announcement are upcoming and are not used as qualifying releases.

**Documented loop.** Choose a profession → explore for its materials → make goods and advance its recipe/tool path → sell products or complete quests → enter further regions and temples → find eggs and raise helpful Lumas → develop the farm and pursue another profession. The official current [press kit](https://www.lumaisland.com/press) describes eight profession paths and tool upgrades. No stamina bar or inventory cap restricts collection; this removes two common reasons for interrupting an excursion. [Developer's store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2408820/Luma_Island/).

**Progression and feedback.** The **Pirates update shipped 20 June 2025**. It added Shipwright work constructing boat pieces to repair a galleon, a Pirate Cove spanning progression, companion abilities, and three modes. Cozy removes mine enemies, makes outside enemies generally non-aggressive unless provoked, reduces costs, and raises drops; Hero adds stronger threats and harsher item loss. A water Luma can find treasure and dig critters, an earth Luma harvests extra crops, and other types assist different tasks. These mode and companion details belong to Pirates, not automatically to the original launch. [Developer's shipped Pirates notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/2408820/announcements/detail/548989708728800603).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** A [12 November 2025 recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198407556555/recommended/2408820/) particularly appreciates unlimited inventory and flexible professions. A [26 December recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197999127578/recommended/2408820/) values exploration without energy or bedtime constraints. A [4 August 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198147987381/recommended/2408820/) finds profession gathering repetitive and character interactions shallow despite enjoying its low-pressure rules. A [6 January 2026 review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198147258879/recommended/2408820/) says its **Vanilla** profession outputs feel interchangeable and useful mostly for money; it explicitly had not yet tested Pirates. That edition boundary matters when interpreting the criticism.

**Tidekin transfer hypothesis and risk.** Keep common salvage easy to gather and store, then place the strategic choice in the raft's working space, machine configuration, and destination. A shipwright-like goal can show each delivered part physically rebuilding a boat. Avoid creating three professions that all reduce to feeding different ingredients into a waiting station for sale. Give their outputs uses in each other's projects: rope routes power, a crafted float changes sensing, food supports an optional longer excursion. Unlimited inventory alone does not ensure those decisions exist.

**Concrete experiment — E6, unlimited salvage versus limited machine space.** Give ten players identical salvage islands and a raft with six machine mounts. Compare an unrestricted materials bag with a small bag requiring returns; retain six mounts in both. Pass if unrestricted storage halves forced unloading trips while seven players can still explain a tradeoff among their mounted machines. Track meaningful reconfiguration separately from sorting time. If choice disappears, deepen machine functions rather than restoring routine inventory interruptions by default.

## 7. Slime Rancher 2 — caring, earning, exploring, and delegating

**Release and traction.** Monomi Park's **A Hero in Time 1.0 shipped 23 September 2025**, completing the main adventure and adding Quantum Drones. The Steam snapshot is 43,886 reviews, 94.5% positive, accumulated since its 2022 Early Access launch. Do not confuse the store's audience claim for the original Slime Rancher with this sequel's success. [1.0 notes](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1657630/announcements/detail/521975626158049415), [Steam metadata](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1657630/Slime_Rancher_2/).

**Documented loop.** Explore Rainbow Island → collect slimes and resources → ranch and farm at the Conservatory → obtain plorts → earn newbucks → expand the Conservatory and improve the vacpack/gadgets → explore more effectively. A creature's output links tending the home to progress away from it. [Developer's store description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1657630/Slime_Rancher_2/).

**Progression and feedback.** 1.0 Quantum Drones have Rancher mode for programmed care/gardening/cleanup and Explorer mode for gathering in the world. Drone Stations program, charge, and fuel them with plorts; gathered resources become accessible through Quantum Cloud inventory. The map exposes location and energy. Thus automation can free exploration time while still presenting a maintenance dependency. The launch notes acknowledge that competing drones can freeze on a shared task. The **2 December 2025** follow-up adds recipe pinning/resource progress and a complete toy collection, generally lowering slime agitation with some special effects. [1.0 systems and known issues](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1657630/announcements/detail/521975626158049415), [1.1 improvements](https://steamcommunity.com/games/1657630/announcements/detail/537745839127265968).

**Player feedback and failure friction.** A [25 April 2026 recommendation](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198275950148/recommended/1657630/) enjoys the collection/care/exploration loop. ## Synthesis for Tidekin

### What the comparisons support—and what remains a hypothesis

| Design relationship worth testing | Primary examples | Tidekin interpretation | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A traveling home keeps crafting near discovery | Aloft, Forever Skies | Salvage immediately changes the raft players live on | A travel failure also disables the workshop and stored progress |
| Environmental conditions power production | Aloft; visible logistics in Oddsparks | Tidal flow is a readable input to mechanisms | Invisible state makes natural variability feel random |
| Discovery adds a capability | Abiotic Factor, Grounded 2 | New parts enable pumping, sensing, towing, or access | Reward becomes a larger number after a long trip |
| Automation releases time for another activity | Slime Rancher 2 | Helpers keep routine raft work running during exploration | Maintenance becomes guilt, or all meaningful interaction vanishes |
| Accumulated work has social or spatial evidence | Dinkum, Tiny Bookshop | Repaired boats, returning visitors, and a visibly improved workshop | Repeated deliveries without changed behavior |
| Low pressure and agency need distinct treatment | Luma Island, Wanderstop | Few handling chores; voluntary rituals; persistent construction | Safety becomes inactivity, or calm presentation conceals loss |

The table groups documented relationships described in the individually cited profiles; it is a comparison, not a success model. In particular, the strong cumulative ratings do not establish retention, completion, current-player counts, or causal preference for any one system.

### Proposed core loop

**Read the sea → choose an excursion → discover a part or problem → assemble a mechanism on the raft → watch it work through a tide change → use its result to open a route, help someone, or improve home → choose the next excursion.**

The loop has three distinct rewards. A part changes what the player can do. A visible mechanism explains how their design works. A lasting place or relationship shows why the work mattered. These are Tidekin hypotheses distilled from the comparisons, not verified features or guarantees.

Use three nested cadences as prototype parameters, not observed benchmark timings:

- **Seconds:** grab a part, connect a cord, turn a valve, observe water or motion. Every manipulation should make its result inspectable.
- **Minutes:** one low-tide visit, one complete assembly, one return-and-unload. Aim for a first functioning invention inside ten minutes.
- **Several sessions:** add a new physical verb, restore a route, and encounter a returning visitor. Prefer a changed possibility over another requirement for the same material.

### Make tides an opportunity with a recovery plan

A tide can alter access, current direction, power, and water head without being a constant deadline. Separate these concepts in the interface: high water is not automatically fast current, and maximum power need not occur at high tide. Decide whether Tidekin uses an intentionally simplified tide model, then teach that model through a ruler, current arrow, and visibly moving machinery. Do not present a physical relationship the game does not simulate.

Before committing to a machine, players should be able to inspect its expected operating range. Prototype distinct explanations for “waiting for current,” “not submerged,” “output full,” and “mechanical obstruction.” Preserve useful output in a tank or flywheel so slack water can become decorating, visiting, or exploration time rather than mandatory waiting. For tide-locked wrecks, offer another reachable activity and a reliable return marker.

Test both players who welcome planning and players who came to relax. If the tide is understood but disliked, better explanation will not fix the cadence. Shorten the cycle, provide a player-controlled waiting option, or reduce the activities that depend on a narrow window.

### Progress through reusable inventions

Start with a small vocabulary: **float, cord, pulley, wheel, valve, container, counterweight, and sensor**. These are proposed prototype parts. Each should have at least two useful applications before adding another material tier. For example, a float can track water level or lift a gate; a counterweight can assist a crane or hold a valve open. Tide reversal then creates a reason to learn a valve, and a larger load creates a reason to combine parts.

Use three progression tracks only if they remain connected:

1. **Capability:** a mechanism enables an action or reach that was impossible before.
2. **Understanding:** players learn to predict and diagnose its behavior with fewer prompts.
3. **Belonging:** a workshop, restored route, or recurring person reflects their work.

Decoration should begin before expensive technology. Allow cheap moves, undo, and recoverable experiments so the raft can evolve without a restructuring tax. Keep recipes pinned and remember discovered material locations. Give automation a specific job to relieve, then assess whether the player uses the released time for something they value.

### Distinguish design friction from loss of control

Useful candidate friction includes choosing a route, trading compactness for power, learning how to deal with reversal, and deciding which request to serve first. These are consequential decisions with interpretable outcomes.

High-risk friction includes repeated container sorting, hidden progression prerequisites, forcing a return solely to learn what a station can craft, mysterious rejection of a valid-looking machine, and destroying the raft or save through a recovery failure. The sampled critiques of Aloft, Forever Skies, Oddsparks, and Tiny Bookshop give specific reasons to test these classes; they do not tell us their population frequency.

Treat recovery as part of the primary loop. A stranded raft needs tow/reset-to-safe-water, a dismantled mechanism needs reusable parts, an exhausted starter economy needs a baseline source of supplies, and an update needs a migration or accessible preserved world. A cozy tone does not compensate for losing hours of committed work.

### Focused experiment order

Run **E3 first** to establish whether players can read the mechanism. Then combine **E1 and E2** to test whether tides create decisions and whether a failure remains recoverable. Run **E4** to establish whether the next excursion offers a new capability, and **E6** to choose the storage/space boundary. Only after those work, add **E5/E10** for social purpose, **E7** for delegation, and **E9** for an optional resting ritual. E8 helps tune salvage recognition and tool controls throughout.

One compact vertical slice can test most of this: a raft, two channels, a tide wheel, a gate, a container, a stranded skiff, and one returning fisher. Proposed slice acceptance criteria:

- Eight of ten new players build a functioning mechanism in ten minutes without a recipe-by-recipe instructor.
- Eight of ten explain why the machine pauses and what the next tide/current change will do.
- No player loses access to a playable raft or needs a new save after the sandbar/power failure.
- Seven of ten can describe a different plan enabled by the first new component.
- Six of ten voluntarily choose a second excursion or a non-paying repair.
- Forced waiting and sorting together consume less than 15% of active session time; optional resting is recorded separately.

These are provisional go/no-go criteria for small formative tests, not statistically validated targets. Record observations and participant explanations as well as counts. Counterbalance comparisons, keep material quantities and travel distances comparable, and distinguish cozy-first from automation-first preferences. A failed criterion should change the relevant mechanism or cadence before increasing content volume.

### Current-loop audit: why the existing numbers do not yet create much choice

This is a **source inspection and arithmetic audit, not a human playtest**. The inspected [game domain](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js) on 8 October 2026 had SHA-256 `d02c4ddebd2d6a94807211908eb6d4a6345df666bc60f48f98208635066586d9`; concurrent implementation may subsequently differ. The [project contract](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/docs/PROJECT.md) promises sailing, tide reading, salvage, five useful upgrades, destinations, and persistent progression.

**The draft provides a clear, recoverable completion path, but its progression is mostly a purchase checklist.** Every recipe requires the preceding upgrade, so the player has one legal next purchase. Builds set fixed capabilities rather than asking the player to configure a mechanism. A greenhouse granting permission to enter a reef needs a visible explanation of how it makes that trip possible. Destination travel is an immediate, free repositioning action, which supports accessibility but weakens voyage planning if it supplies the whole travel loop. [Recipes and build rules](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:106), [travel](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:238).

The numerical consequences are unusually clear:

- **180-second tides:** water height follows `(1 - cos(2πt/180))/2`. The `height <= 0.35` surface-salvage rule exposes tidal nodes for **72.5 seconds per cycle**, leaving **107.5 seconds** inaccessible from the surface. Diving bypasses that rule at any tide. Thus a missed window can produce a long wait, while the alternate mode makes timing unnecessary for a player comfortable diving. This can be a useful dry-versus-wet choice, but the game must present the alternatives. [Access rule](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:148), [height model](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:391).
- **Power:** the wheel produces **0.6–1.4 energy/second**, averaging 1.0. Crane and greenhouse together consume **4/12 + 3/24 = 0.458 energy/second**. Even minimum generation exceeds their combined average demand; minimum generation over 24 seconds is 14.4, against 11 consumed. The tide changes the meter's filling speed rather than requiring an ongoing operating decision. Power is based on water height, not current velocity. [Machine production](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:395).
- **Automatic salvage:** crane output is **10 wood + 5 scrap/minute**; greenhouse adds **7.5 wood/minute**. Both together supply **17.5 wood + 5 scrap/minute**, indefinitely, without depleting nearby debris. Five recipes collectively cost **44 wood, 27 scrap, and 9 pearls**, before objective subsidies. Common-material collection therefore becomes optional after automation; that is only useful if the released time has another compelling job. [Recipes](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:106), [machine yields](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:395).
- **Exploration can be skipped:** home contains four pearls. First-dive, crane, greenhouse, and sail objectives award **1 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 5 more**, meeting the complete nine-pearl upgrade budget without claiming either visit objective. Starting resources, home salvage, early rewards, and subsequent automation supply the common materials. Therefore **all five upgrades can be built while staying in the home region**. This is an existence proof from the economy, not an observed player route. [Home nodes](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:80), [objective rewards](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:260).
- **18-second dives:** oxygen depletes at one unit/second, manual surfacing is always available, and exhaustion returns the player home with inventory preserved plus six seconds of recovery. A dive may begin with only six oxygen remaining, so its available time is not always 18 seconds. These forgiving recovery rules are appropriate for the stated tone; the timer needs an optional second target or route decision to create purpose. Raising the penalty would not supply that decision. [Dive actions](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:350), [recovery](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/game.js:438).

The existing interface already pauses simulation in menus and on focus loss, displays oxygen seconds, and warns below six seconds. Preserve those protections. The critique is about what players choose during active time, not a need to make menus dangerous. [Oxygen feedback](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/main.js:383), [simulation pause](/Users/jacobusbrink/Jaxs/projects/tidekin/src/main.js:556).

### Scope decisions and unanswered questions

**Recommended first commitment:** preserve the raft, make tide-powered mechanisms diagnosable, and ensure the first expedition returns with a part that opens a new possibility. Avoid building a broad economy or many creature/profession variants before that relationship works.

Evidence still needed from Tidekin itself:

- Whether alternating tides feel like welcome planning or scheduling pressure.
- Whether players enjoy physical assembly repeatedly after they understand it.
- Whether raft size limits prompt interesting layouts or prevent desired expression.
- Whether visitors make the ocean feel inhabited with a small writing budget.
- Whether delegation frees exploration time while leaving satisfying work at home.
- Whether the emotional promise is durable creative refuge, dangerous survival, or a selectable combination.

No mechanic in this report is established as the cause of another game's success. The deliverable is a sourced set of design relationships, failure cases, and experiments that can establish which of them actually belong in Tidekin.
